Data Analyst Intern Education Team Reporting Automation

✓ Delivered & handed over

Every week, the Education Team rebuilt the same report by hand: export from Zoho CRM, clean it in Excel, compile, share. By the time it landed it was already out of date. My job was to make that report build itself, and to make the numbers trustworthy enough that people would actually use it.

01 ExtractPython on AWS Lambda, run weekly by EventBridge, pulling deals from the Zoho CRM API with incremental loads. Most of the real work was debugging: OAuth scopes, module and endpoint names, pagination, and API field names that didn’t match what staff saw on screen.
02 CleanFlattened nested owner and contact fields, standardised dates and states, and wrote deduplication logic (latest record per booking, by ingestion timestamp) so repeated loads never double count revenue.
03 ModelS3, a Glue catalogue and Athena, with 4 reusable SQL views (deals_clean, deals_latest, deals_simplified_v2, an owner view) as a stable layer between raw data and the dashboard.
04 ShowA multi-page QuickSight dashboard: KPI cards and year-on-year trend, revenue by stage and by state, a program-details table for checking records, quarter views and a sales matrix, all filterable by year, owner, state and stage.

Business Analysis & Market Research

Saint Ann’s School NSW Market Feasibility

✓ Completed · Client project

Where should a school open its first NSW campus? Three phases, 2,500+ suburbs, one recommendation, every step backed by evidence the client could check.

RoleBusiness Analyst in a 6-person team. Led data analysis in Phase 3 and helped build the scoring method and client presentations.
DidChose SA2 as the unit (a real community, not a street or a whole council) and picked indicators that each tie to a business reason: income, children, education and occupation. Then scored 2,500+ areas two independent ways, an Excel pass count against clear thresholds and a Python weighted ranking to separate the close calls.
CheckedThe two methods agreed on about 90% of the shortlist, so we trusted it. We also wrote down what the data couldn’t tell us yet (transport, competition, land), and that list became the Phase 3 test across 3 clusters.
ResultRecommended Lower North Shore. It wasn’t the wealthiest cluster (Upper North Shore was, at 20.8%) and it had the most competitors (5 schools). We chose it for scale (8,639 households), a community more open to change, and gaps in the age ranges existing schools cover. Delivered across 3 formal presentations.
